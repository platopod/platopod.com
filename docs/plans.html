Plans in words

Room UMLYNV. An example of where it fits: a Year 9 Digital Technologies unit on algorithms. No notebook and no code: the plan is written in plain English.

An algorithm is steps in order. Here the steps are sentences, the pod carries them out, and the order shows the moment it is wrong.

What students do

  1. Join at arena.platopod.com/join with the code UMLYNV and a name, and choose the group.
  2. Click Open your pod's screen. The box at the bottom takes the plan; it starts with an example.
  3. Write where the pod should go, in order. For example: Drive to the bottom left corner, then the top right corner, then the centre. Then stop.
  4. Click Make a plan. The plan comes back as numbered steps: read it, because that is what will run. If the words could mean two things, it asks a question instead.
  5. The pod sets off at once, with no Start to press. Swap two steps, make the plan again, and see what changes.

What a plan can say

  • Places: the corners (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right), the centre, the middle of each side (top, bottom, left, right), or a point in metres.
  • Moves: go to a place, patrol places in turn, circle, sweep an area, follow another pod, keep away from one, wait, stop.
  • Speed: slow, normal or fast.

Sharing the room

Each visitor's plan drives their own pod and nobody else's. A new plan replaces your last one; the other pods carry on with theirs. The room takes six people at once, and a pod nobody has used for 20 minutes leaves, which frees its place.

How the words become a plan

A language model turns the words into a plan. Before anything moves, the plan is checked against the arena and read back in plain sentences; the model never drives a pod. There is a limit on how many plans one person can ask for in a minute: if it says to wait, wait a minute.

In a room of your own, the Plain English activity takes the same plans: each student writes one for their own pod, and the teacher's Run starts them together.
