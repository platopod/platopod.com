Three versions, one idea.

From the start, Plato Pod has been about one camera over a table of small robots, with software filling in the rest of the world. What that world contains has grown with each version.

1

A camera that plays every sensor

KoreaTech, South Korea

The first platform was built at the Korea University of Technology and Education to make programming classes less about memorising a language and more about making something move. Off-the-shelf robots ran in a table-top arena with real walls and real obstacles. One overhead camera watched the whole table, and a computer-vision algorithm turned what it saw into the robots' sensors, so a line follower, a maze, a vacuum cleaner and a game of tag could all be set without extra hardware. The robots ran on the Robot Operating System and were reached over the network, so students could program them from anywhere.

Team: Junho Heo, Dongyun Kim and Junsu Park, with Artem Lensky. Published as “Educational platform for learning programming via controlling mobile robots”, IEEE Xplore.

2

Robots of our own

KoreaTech, South Korea

The second platform replaced the off-the-shelf robots with a differential-drive robot designed in-house, 9 cm across and 6 cm high, driven over a Bluetooth link. A mini PC became the server, and the arena became something to assemble, with the camera on its own post above it.

Team: Konho Park, Kyongjae Kim, Jongyoun Lim, Raminta Olbutaite and Artem Lensky.

3

A world around the robots

UNSW Canberra, Australia

At UNSW Canberra the platform became what it is today. The pods shrank to 85 mm across; each has an ESP32 board and is driven over Bluetooth. The arena no longer needs walls or markers: a pod drives a small circle to show the camera where the table is, the arena is drawn on the screen, and the obstacles are virtual. A simulated world grew around the robots: sensors, hazards, virtual units and an opposing force. It runs on a server built on ROS 2, is shown on ATAK, and is used for teaching and for rehearsal.

Built by Artem Lensky and Rhianne Chng. See About for today's team.
