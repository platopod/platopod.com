Used two ways.

The same arena serves a staff exercise and a lecture course. The seam between them is narrow on purpose: one is the scenario, the other is the function that decides what a unit does inside it.

Wargaming

Blue and red force, rules of engagement and an autonomous OPFOR driven by a behaviour tree. Exercises are written as configuration — capture the flag, cordon and search, CBRN reconnaissance patrol — and virtual units spawn alongside the physical pods, so a few robots stand in for a force larger than the desk. Positions and engagement events bridge to ATAK, so the exercise appears where the rest of the picture already does.

Teaching

Route following is a pure-pursuit controller with its integral and derivative terms exposed, so a lecture on PID has something that visibly oscillates when the gains are wrong. Pursuit and evasion, planning through obstacles, and task allocation between cooperating robots are each one or two pure functions with tests that run without a robot — a student writes a strategy, not a stack, and watches it lose to someone else's on the floor.
