Real robots. Virtual force. One arena.

Small robots on a table or the floor, with a world built around them in software: virtual units, sensors, hazards and an opposing force. Students program them. Officers rehearse with them.

Teaching Students write code, or orders in plain English, and watch a real robot carry them out. From a school class with no code to final-year work on control and planning. Military Rehearse on a table against an opposing force that fights back, under rules of engagement that are enforced, with the picture on ATAK over real ground.

What your students or cadets will do.

  • Program a real robot

    A pod on the table does what the code says, in Python or any other language, or in plain English with no code at all.

  • Share one arena as a class

    Twenty students join from their laptops, each with a pod of their own, on the table in the room or on our hosted arena from any browser.

  • Rehearse against an opposing force

    Virtual units, simulated sensors, gas and weapons fire under rules of engagement, with the picture on ATAK laid over real ground.

Two ways to run it.

In your room, or from any browser.

In your room. A camera and a projector on a tripod, a small computer and the pods. No markers to lay out, no walls to build, nothing to install.

  1. 1

    Switch it on

    The box starts by itself and opens its own Wi-Fi. Join it from any laptop and the console opens in a browser.

  2. 2

    Let a pod find the table

    Put a pod under the camera and press Calibrate. It drives one small circle, and from that the system knows where the table lies.

  3. 3

    Draw the arena

    Click two corners of a rectangle on the screen, or trace another shape. The pods stay inside it.

From any browser. Nothing in the room but the laptops. The arenas run on our server. A teacher's account is by invitation; the people joining need no account at all.

  1. 1

    Make a room

    Sign in and make a room: a class, a workshop or a rehearsal. It gets a six-letter code and an arena for each group.

  2. 2

    Hand out the code

    Each person types the code and their name and gets a pod of their own, with its colour. The same name gets the same pod back.

  3. 3

    Run the exercise

    The group's arena is on the projector or in 3D on the big screen. Every exercise briefs the room, keeps its rules and marks the result. Today the hosted arena runs virtual pods; real pods from a laptop's webcam are being tested.
