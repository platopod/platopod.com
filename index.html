Augmented-reality tactical simulation

Physical robots and virtual force share one arena.

Plato Pod puts real robots, simulated units, sensor effects and engagement rules into the same space, at the same time. A pod that drives across a desk is fighting an adversary that is not there, over terrain that is not there, under rules that are.

Capability

A tactical layer, not a robot demo

The robots are the cheap part. What makes an exercise worth running is everything the robots cannot see.

Engagement

Fire, damage, health

A central evaluator resolves every shot against weapon characteristics, line of sight and the target's state. Damage degrades mobility and the ability to return fire.

Rules of engagement

Weapons hold, tight, free

Fire is checked against the posture in force, with civilian and friendly checks applied before a round is ever resolved.

Line of sight

Terrain that blocks

Visibility is evaluated against cover and elevation — by a fast 2D ray model, or by a Gazebo lidar backend behind the same interface.

Electronic warfare

Jamming and dead zones

Emitters, jammed regions and communications dead zones are part of the world state, so a unit can be cut off without being touched.

Sensing

Ten sensor models

Gas with stateful detector dynamics, lidar, sonar, thermal, rangefinder, IED detection, direction finding, GPS, UAV camera and friend-or-foe.

Interoperability

Cursor on Target

Positions, zones, sensor readings and engagement events bridge to ATAK, so an exercise appears where the rest of the picture already does.

Scale

The same platform on a desk and in a field

Only the localisation changes. The tactical layer above it — engagement, sensing, rules, logistics — is identical, so a scenario rehearsed on a tabletop is the scenario you take outside.

For classrooms

Every scenario is an exercise with one seam

The same platform is used for teaching. Each scenario's behaviour is a pure function with tests that run without the robotics stack, so a student replaces a strategy, not an architecture.

  • chase and flee — decide a heading each tick for pursuit and evasion.
  • follow — turn a route and a pose into a velocity.
  • assign_targets — decide which robot searches where. This single function is the whole of the cooperation in the sweep, and the obvious first exercise.

Teams can be compared directly: the console times each run and keeps the best, so a class competes on a measured result rather than an impression.
