Wargaming on a table.

A handful of real robots, a force of virtual units around them, and the rules that decide who sees whom and what a shot does. Officers can rehearse on the desk and watch the same exercise on ATAK, the Android Team Awareness Kit.

A mixed force

Every unit is blue or red and has a role, from a soldier to a tank. Virtual units are spawned alongside the pods and read the same world, so two robots on the desk can stand in for a much larger force.

An autonomous opposing force

The opposing force needs no operator. It patrols, observes, engages and withdraws, deciding ten times a second from what it can see.

Fire, cover and rules of engagement

  • EngagementEvery shot is resolved against the weapon, line of sight, cover, visibility and the target's armour.
  • DamageScaled by armour, in a deliberately simple model: one hit stops a soldier, four a tank. A damaged unit slows and fires less well, however it is driven.
  • Weapons controlHold, tight or free, checked before each shot is resolved, as part of the rules of engagement.
  • StrikesCalled by the umpire on one unit or an area.

A gas hazard on the desk

An umpire can release a gas plume or shift the wind mid-exercise. The pods' gas sensors read the change, with the lag of a real detector, and ATAK redraws the plume.

Exercises written as files

An exercise is a file that sets out the ground, the forces, the hazards and the rules. Nine ship with the platform, among them capture the flag, cordon and search, and a CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear) reconnaissance patrol. Writing a new one needs no code.

The picture on ATAK

Positions, zones, sensor readings, engagements and casualties go out as Cursor on Target, so the exercise appears on ATAK, scaled up and laid over a real map.

Plain English, for officers

The plain-English track lets an officer give the pods orders in words rather than code. Stating intent precisely enough for a robot to act on it is a skill, and the arena shows where an order was unclear.

Beyond the desk

The tactical layer is designed to work with dismounted units carrying GNSS as well as with pods under a camera. Positions come in through one interface, whatever their source. The GNSS side of that interface is not written yet.
