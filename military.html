Wargaming on a table or the floor.

Real pods and virtual units in one arena, with line of sight, rules of engagement, damage and an autonomous opposing force. Each exercise is a file, so a new one needs no code, and every exercise can be followed on ATAK, laid over a real map.

Exercises, simplest first

1 · Search

Gas plume search

Find a hidden gas source with simulated gas sensors that respond with a real detector's lag. The umpire can release gas or shift the wind mid-exercise.

2 · Contest

Capture the flag

Two teams, their zones and a flag, laid out among obstacles. One version maps the arena onto a real sports field at 1:150, so ATAK shows the game on the field.

3 · Surveillance

Get through unseen

One side places sensors and commands a patrol; the other gets its pods across undetected, using cover. Detection falls with range and is blocked by cover. Orders are written in plain English.

4 · Patrol

Patrol

A lap round the arena on time, reporting what changed since the last one: a unit on a route, the referee noting what it saw and when it was back.

5 · Reconnaissance

CBRN recon patrol

A team advances to find the source of a chemical release and report it, with gas and thermal sensors, while a small enemy element may interdict.

6 · Built-up area

Cordon and search

Cordon a cluster of buildings and search it for IEDs with a simulated detector. Civilians are inside, so any shot near the buildings is flagged under the rules of engagement, and a sniper waits in cover.

7 · Defence

Defensive position

Hold a perimeter against an autonomous opposing force, with indirect fire called by the umpire on a unit or an area.

8 · Joint

Joint force coordination

Two blue platoons coordinate over ATAK. An observer watches through a simulated UAV camera on a virtual aircraft and calls in a strike, and a direction-finding receiver takes bearings on an enemy emitter.

No code

Orders in plain English

Officers give the pods orders in words. Each plan is turned into a fixed set of orders (go to, patrol, sweep, follow, keep away, wait, stop) and read back before anything moves, so an unclear order shows before it is carried out.

The rules of the world

  • ForcesEvery unit is blue or red, with a role from soldier to tank. Virtual units read the same world as the pods, so two robots can stand in for a larger force.
  • Opposing forceAutonomous. It patrols, observes, engages and withdraws, deciding ten times a second from what it can see.
  • Line of sightChecked against cover, visibility and weather.
  • EngagementEvery shot is resolved against the weapon, line of sight, cover, visibility and the target's armour.
  • DamageScaled by armour, in a deliberately simple model: one hit stops a soldier, four a tank. A damaged unit slows and fires less well, however it is driven.
  • Weapons controlHold, tight or free, checked before each shot is resolved, as part of the rules of engagement.
  • UmpireReleases gas, shifts the wind, or calls a strike on a unit or an area, mid-exercise. Sensors and ATAK see the change.
  • SensorsEleven, simulated, each with adjustable noise: gas, GNSS, sonar, radar, lidar, thermal, rangefinder, IED detector, direction finding, UAV camera and friend-or-foe.
  • ATAKPositions, zones, sensor readings, engagements and casualties go out as Cursor on Target, scaled up and laid over a real map.
  • ExercisesA file sets out the ground, the forces, the hazards and the rules. Nine ship with the platform.
  • RefereeEvery scenario carries a brief, a debrief with the result and marks, and rules for who plays, how an order may reach a unit while the run is on, and how long. The server ends a run whose time is up.
  • ViewsThe controller's console, a top-down picture for a projector over the table, and a 3D view of the arena for the big screen, all from one feed.
  • Where it runsOn a box of the unit's own, on its own Wi-Fi and no other network, or hosted and reached over TLS from any browser.
  • OutdoorsThe tactical layer is designed for dismounted units carrying GNSS as well as pods under a camera. That interface is not written yet.
